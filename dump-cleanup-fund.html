    1. Subtract the attacker's DMG from the target's current Armour Pips.
    2. Damage dealt reduces Pips and any excess hits HP. Pips
    3. If DMG > Pips, roll a D12 using standard resolution. On a failure the armour breaks - reducing your pips to 0, otherwise it can't be reduced below 1 Pip.

1 naming conventions

Starborn
  • Names are Exonyms (given by others for identification and familiarity).
  • Names<Titles. Concepts>Function. Prefix "Aelf". Material can play a role.
    • Ex.:
    • Aelf-Obsidian,
    • Aelf-Artifice, or The Artificer.
    • The Apex
    • Aelf-Silence
    • Aelf-Dogma
    • The Thing
Ar'darun
  • Names are industrial and purpose-driven.
  • Jobs and assignments over substance. Colour or other distinct features can play a role. Sometimes longer words erode into nicks (can also signal affection or independence from the Spire).
    • Ex.:
    • Tink (from Tinker)
    • Runner
    • Jane (from Janitor)
    • Rad (from Radio)
    • Cass (from Casing/Casting)
Daemons
  • They are defined by their fall. They have titles that need to be claimed through action, declarations that need fulfillment to count.
    • Ex.:
    • Malice
    • Sorrow
    • Alienation
    • Murder
    • Husk
    • The Demagogue
    • The Heretic
    • The Usurper
Yigvrese (Yig)
  • Names related to minerals, ore, metals, using many harsh and hard sounds (like G, R, K, Z, X, Y).
  • Tough to pronounce for everyone else, so socialite Yig names erode into softer, easier to pronounce and remember names, "Kryzx" may become "Clicks".
    • Ex.:
    • Krakk (Crack)
    • Zygax
    • Grux (Cruz)
    • Xyrok (Rock)
    • Kygroz
  • Yigvrese being the "Official" name for their kind, similarly to "Homo Sapiens", rarely sees use. The representatives refer to their kin as a shortened Yigg, which was adapted widely for being less of a mouthful. Reducing them to a terms such as "Diggers" may come off as mean.
Forestfolk
  • Scandinavian/Swedish names, particularly ones ending in -son, putting emphasis on their ancestry and elders.
    • Ex.:
    • Hemingson
    • Ragisson
    • Gamlisson
    • Anson
    • Paulsen
    • Kristoffersen
    • Ottosen
    • Olofsonn
  • Calling them Furries drives them crazy, in the wrong sense. Also disrespect to lineage is non-tolerable (omitting the -son).
Houndvari
  • Either directly descriptive names alluding to their traits or appearance, or common names shared between humans and domesticated animals
    • Ex.:
    • Daisy
    • Diego
    • Fluffer
    • Paws
    • Wagger
    • Chaser
    • Cupcake
  • They seem to have a deaf ear for any negative nicks or insults thrown their way, so long as they know they're the ones being addressed.
Fireborn
  • True names are stereotypically fire-related (ex. Coal, Spark, Bright), but those living in Sanctuaries often adapt a common first name to pair it with, resulting in a first name + surname structure
    • Ex.:
    • Bob Spark
    • Norman Bright
    • Ada Burns
    • Frank Coal
    • John Wick
  • They may treat being addressed as their True names as insulting or endearing depending on the speaker and surroundings.

Spires

Neohuman Spires and Corps

The Spires are not united. They share a goal (profit and domination) but are cutthroat, even with each other. They engage in open fights over resources or sabotage. However, they must collaborate, as their services are essential to each other and expensive.

  • Longhorn and MGM - McGriff Mining. Provide materials for the others, at harsh prices.
  • (Destroyed) Derelict and Bröninger. Scattered throught the Spires, making weaponry and processing materials from McGriff.
  • Brighthouse and CMT - Control, Maintain, Transport. Control The Ledger, and hold the others back with benefits from all three, as everyone understands the need for solidarity, at least on the surface.
    • RF - Reclamation Forces. High-tech pest control / Intrusion specialists / hazardous materials crew / critical asset extraction team. They're not there to "Win", but to solve the issue at hand, even if its putting a Band-Aid on exposed bone.
      • Vs Overgrowth - Purge Squads. Equipped with flamers and chemical defoliants.
      • Vs The Bad Moon - Anchor Squads. They can't fight The Moon, but they will bolt themselves to the ground and fire as many Shell Piercing rounds as it takes to protect their escort if need be.
      • Vs The Deep - Sanity Squads. More of an intel and precaution branch as once the effects set in, the location is lost anyway.
      • Vs Shimmer - Disposal Squads. Tasked with locating and relocating the Heart, while breaking as much of the surface as they can on their way there.
    • RF - Reclamation Corps. The subtler twin handling records that need erasing and mouths that should have really stopped talking a few sentences ago.
  • Cloudline and US - Updraft Systems. Secretly hostile towards McGriff and responsible for Air Superiority near Spire territory.
    Economy: "NeoMoney" and the Role of CMT
  • Currency: NeoHumans have their own "currency" that doesn't exist elsewhere. It's not cash, but an internal ledger system based on a "complex equation".
    • The Equation: Value is determined by CMT and based on raw materials, technology, research, assets, workforce, and personal scores to settle.
  • CMT's Control: CMT (the "Control" faction) literally controls the value of everything. They are the central bank and the police, rolled into one. This "currency" is their leash. They can arbitrarily devalue a Spire's industry (e.g., heavy machinery, hurting Longhorn) to keep everyone in check.
  • External: The currency is worthless to outsiders. Trade with the rest of Jaddivar is done via barter of the actual goods (guns, materials, transport).
    Co-dependent Animosity
  • The Delicate Balance: CMT is the head, but it can't afford to kill the body (Longhorn, Updraft) that feeds it. This creates a fragile cooperativeness.
  • The Secret: This internal hatred and co-dependence is the Spires' most closely guarded secret, worth billions of NeoMoney, and heavily censored by Starchart.
  • Conflict: This is a cold war fought with spreadsheets, sabotage, and corporate espionage. Conflicts are "aggressive asset acquisitions". Lives lost are just logged "manpower," a replaceable asset in the grand equation.
    • Adventurers are not in any books, unaccounted for, not evaluated, and completely deniable - making them the perfect solution for desperate situations.
    • Groups that do regular work for a Spire can get standing with it, represented with gear, trust, and better work for better pay, but it can all fall apart at one wrong step as the execs pull out. Rival Spires can also consider you an "on-sight" threat at the higher levels of standing.
      • Double crossing a Spire will have CMT send out a squad for both the assets that still need recovering, and the traitorous adventurers.

Faction Relations

  • Spires vs. Saurians:
    • Relationship: Actively Hostile. Open conflict.
    • Saurian Perspective: Spires are an existential threat, a plague, arrogant as the Starborn.
    • Spire Perspective: Saurians are "hostile indigenous fauna," an obstacle, a "Class-B Threat" that's just sitting on resource nodes.
  • Spires vs. Sanctuary Cities:
    • Relationship: Tense, Pragmatic Neutrality. Purely transactional.
    • Dynamic: The "Switzerland" of Jaddivar. "Check your wars at the gate".
    • Sanctuary Perspective: NeoHumans are awful, but their barter (tech, arms) is good.
    • Spire Perspective: Useful, if annoying. CMT hates the lack of control, but a neutral hub is needed for trade and hiring deniable assets.
    • Speculation: The only place a Longhorn manager and a Yig clan-chief might negotiate. Spires pay exorbitant fees for this neutrality and hate it.
  • Yig vs. McGriff:
    • Relationship: Grudging Professional Rivals.
    • Dynamic: They are in the exact same business (underground operations).
    • McGriff's Perspective: Yig are a nuisance ("hyper-intelligent gophers") literally eating their profit. BUT... they are uncanny prospectors. McGriff would contract Yig clans (paying in refined or non-local metals) to better locate veins or caverns.
    • Yig's Perspective: McGriff is loud, sloppy, and wasteful (destroys the food). BUT... they crack open geodes the Yig can't, and sometimes they share.
    • Speculation: Pure cutthroat business. Turf wars, sabotage, but also a functional trade relationship.
  • Spires vs. Forestfolk:
    • Spire Perspective: An obstacle. "Primitives" squatting on valuable lumber and land. To be ignored, "pacified" (bought off with cheap tech), or "reclaimed" (forcibly removed by CMT).
    • Forestfolk Perspective: A plague. To be avoided, repelled, and sabotaged if they encroach. A danger to their lineages and forests.
  • Spires vs. Fireborn:
    • Spire Perspective: A high-value, high-risk industrial asset. A being that is fire? McGriff and Bröninger would kill to have a regular Fireborn workforce.
    • Fireborn Perspective: Deeply suspicious. They were already created as a slave-labour race (by the Starborn). An "employment" offer in a Neo forge sounds eerily familiar...
  • Spires vs. Axi & Meso Humans:
    • Spire Perspective: "Lost cousins," failures. Axi are "deluded" (wasting tech on isolation). Meso are "backwards" (a prime market for surplus guns, source of unskilled labour).
    • Axi & Meso Perspective: "The reason we left." Axi: Spires are "the mistake," repeating Earth's corporate ecocide. Meso: Arrogant, dangerous traders, not to be trusted for everyone's sake.
  • Spires vs. Ar'Darun:
    • Spire Perspective: A proprietary, peak-efficiency workforce. Seen as their creation. The most valuable manpower asset a Spire can own, count as multiple units of "manpower".
    • Ar'Darun Perspective: "We're the ones who actually run this place." They know they were created, but they also know their skills are irreplaceable. A complex relationship of resentment and high-paid privilege.
  • Spires vs. Houndvari:
    • Spire Perspective: Specialized, non-human labor. A Goodlad is a born bodyguard, a Corge is a courier, a Pitmaw is expendable muscle. A resource to be "acquired".
    • Houndvari Perspective: "A job's a job." Pragmatic. Not for everyone, that's for sure.
  • Spires vs. Starborn:
    • Spire Perspective: A myth. Or the ultimate prize. The researchers who know they're real are terrified at the thought.
    • Starborn Perspective: "Unnatural." Like ants squabbling over sunlight. They do not believe Jaddivar's resources can ever be exhausted, but they distrust the methods (machinery, explosives, forest clearing) the Neo employ anyway.
